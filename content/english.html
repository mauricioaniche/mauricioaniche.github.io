03 / AI and agentic software engineering

Building with AI.
Thinking like an engineer.

Explore my AI writing (Portuguese) ↗

Writing code has become easier. Deciding what to build and making sure it works still demand a great deal of engineering.

I use coding agents extensively, and I see our work moving up a level of abstraction. We give agents context, make architecture and product decisions, and judge whether a change solves the right problem. Engineers need to understand the system as a whole, weigh trade-offs, and say no when an agent introduces unnecessary complexity or breaks a fundamental constraint.

For me, agentic software engineering means designing a development process in which agents can plan, implement, test, review, and help operate software, with people setting direction and deciding where human judgment is needed. The quality of that process determines how much we can delegate. Tests, static analysis, observability, and safe deployment and rollback mechanisms give us the feedback we need to trust changes.

That is why I’m interested in software factories: workflows that connect agents across the delivery cycle. Faster code generation can simply move the queue to review, deployment, or operations. We need to redesign the whole flow and use AI to help keep software running in production. The goal is working software and better outcomes for the business.

Computer science and software engineering fundamentals remain essential. They help us frame problems, spot architectural mistakes, and evaluate what agents produce. We also need to preserve our critical thinking: accepting an answer without questioning it means giving up the judgment that makes an engineer valuable.

Adoption is a leadership and learning challenge, too. Leaders need to provide direction, tools, and guardrails while giving teams room to experiment and share what they learn. Costs deserve the same attention: choose models to fit the task and assess the return across the delivery process. I’m excited about what we can already do, and I’m still experimenting, learning, and sharing open questions.
